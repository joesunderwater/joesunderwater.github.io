In a golf simulator bay, the projector usually sits close to the hitting area, so a short-throw lens matters as much as brightness. The Optoma GT1080HDR is a compact home-entertainment and gaming projector with a 0.49:1 throw ratio, 3,800 lumens, and Full HD 1080p resolution. Who it's for

The GT1080HDR is for anyone who needs a bright short-throw 1080p picture in a small room — movies, sports, console or PC gaming, or a golf simulator enclosure. Optoma rates Enhanced Gaming Mode as low as 8.4 ms of input lag, which helps projected shots and games feel more immediate. Native resolution is 1080p, not 4K; the projector is HDR-compatible and can accept high-resolution sources, including UHD from a PC, and display them at 1080p.

You will still need to match the throw and aspect ratio to your screen. Many golf enclosures use a 4:3 impact screen, while this projector’s native aspect ratio is 16:9 (with 4:3 compatibility). Measure the screen and use Optoma’s throw figures rather than assuming any sim bay will be a perfect fit.

Key features

Details below come from Optoma’s official U.S. GT1080HDR product specifications:

  • DLP projector with native 1080p Full HD (1920 x 1080) resolution and a 16:9 native aspect ratio; 4:3 and 16:9 compatible.
  • 3,800 lumens brightness, measured in accordance with ISO 21118, and a listed contrast ratio of 50,000:1.
  • Short-throw lens with a 0.49:1 throw ratio. Optoma says you can project a 120-inch image from about 4.5 feet; projection distance is listed from 15.75 to 133.86 inches, and screen size from 36.2 to 307.8 inches diagonal.
  • Enhanced Gaming Mode with a listed response time as low as 8.4 ms, and a 120 Hz refresh-rate option in that context.
  • HDR-compatible: it can receive and display HDR metadata. Inputs include HDMI 2.0, HDMI 1.4a with 3D support and MHL, VGA, and 3.5 mm audio, plus a 3.5 mm audio output, USB-A power (1.5A) for a dongle such as a Chromecast, and RS-232.
  • Lamp light source (240 W) with rated life up to 4,000 hours in Bright, 10,000 in Eco, 12,000 in Dynamic, and 15,000 in Eco+.
  • Vertical keystone correction of +/- 40 degrees, a 10-watt built-in speaker, and a listed size of 12.44 x 9.61 x 4.25 inches at 6.61 lb net weight.

How to use it

Place the projector in front of the screen at a distance that matches the 0.49:1 throw ratio and your image size. In a golf bay, that usually means mounting or standing it just ahead of the hitting area, high or offset enough that you do not walk through the beam or cast a large shadow on the screen. Connect a computer, launch-monitor software, or media device over HDMI, then set the aspect ratio and keystone so the picture fits the impact screen.

If you use the projector for games or simulator software, try Enhanced Gaming Mode when you want the lowest listed lag. For movies, a standard picture mode and, when the source supports it, HDR metadata will be more appropriate. Use the USB-A port if you need to power a streaming dongle, and rely on the built-in speaker only as a convenience; a separate sound system will fill a garage more evenly.

Give the lamp ventilation space, and use Eco or Eco+ if you want to stretch lamp life.
