Logitech G Pro Flight Multi Panel – Autopilot & Flaps Control Panel for Flight Simulator

Once you start flying longer routes in a PC flight simulator, the autopilot becomes the part of the cockpit you touch most, and clicking tiny on-screen knobs with a mouse gets old fast. The Logitech G Pro Flight Multi Panel moves those controls onto your desk: a USB autopilot controller with a real-time LED display, dedicated autopilot function buttons, and physical controls for flaps, auto throttle, and pitch trim.

Who it's for

The Multi Panel is aimed at home pilots who already have a stick or yoke and want the next layer of a cockpit: hands-on autopilot control. Logitech describes it as working with major PC flight sim software, with a small plug-in from its support page adding compatibility for Microsoft Flight Simulator, FSX, X-Plane, and Prepar3D. It is a PC panel; Logitech's materials list Windows 7, 8.1, and 10.

It is especially useful if you fly airliners or larger aircraft where autopilot heading, altitude, and vertical speed are a big part of each flight. Keep in mind that not every aircraft in a sim has every autopilot function. Logitech's own guide notes that smaller aircraft may not have an autothrottle, so that switch will have no effect in those planes.

Key features

Details below come from Logitech G's product page and the official Flight Multi Panel user guide:

  • Real-time LED display that shows autopilot values while you fly, so you do not need to zoom in on the virtual cockpit.
  • Autopilot function buttons: AP (master on/off), HDG (heading), NAV (NAV1 frequency or GPS), IAS (indicated airspeed), ALT (altitude hold), VS (vertical speed), APR (approach to the runway localizer), and REV (back course).
  • Autopilot settings selector on the left and a settings adjustment wheel to the right of the display for dialing in values.
  • Auto throttle arm switch, so the autopilot can manage engine power to hold a selected speed in aircraft that support it.
  • Flaps switch to retract (up) or extend (down).
  • Pitch trim wheel with resistance, which Logitech says helps you hold your desired attitude.
  • Modular mounting: a supplied bracket lets you stack the panel with other Logitech G flight panels or place it side by side. Owners of the Logitech G Flight Yoke System can mount it on top of the yoke using the included screws.
  • USB connection with drivers and plug-ins available from Logitech's support site.

How to use it

Start by fixing the panel to its mounting bracket, or onto your Logitech G yoke if you have one. Before plugging it in, download the current driver and software from Logitech's Multi Panel support page, then connect the USB cable only when the installer prompts you. If your sim needs a plug-in, install that too; Logitech's guide walks through the prompts FSX shows the first time it loads the panel software.

In the air, the workflow is simple. Turn the selector on the left to the value you want to change, such as ALT, then turn the adjustment wheel to set it. Repeat for IAS and HDG. To engage, arm the auto throttle if your aircraft has one, press the HDG, IAS, and ALT buttons, then press AP. The aircraft then climbs or descends, turns, and holds speed.

Use the flaps switch on approach and the trim wheel for fine pitch adjustments when hand-flying.

Quick FAQ

Does it work with Microsoft Flight Simulator and X-Plane?

Logitech says a small plug-in from its support page adds compatibility with PC flight sims including Microsoft Flight Simulator, FSX, X-Plane, and Prepar3D. Check Logitech's support page for the latest software for your sim version.

Does it work on Xbox?

Logitech lists it for Windows PC. Console support is not mentioned in the official materials, so treat it as a PC accessory.

Can I mount it on my yoke?

Yes, if you own the Logitech G Flight Yoke System. The panel and bracket can be mounted on top of the yoke unit with the screws provided.

Why does the auto throttle switch do nothing in some planes?

Not all aircraft include an autothrottle. In those planes, using the switch has no effect on the autopilot.
