A short-throw projector lets you put a large image on an impact screen without placing the unit at the back of the room, where someone might walk through the beam. The BenQ MW632ST is a WXGA business projector with 3,200 ANSI lumens, a 0.72–0.87 throw ratio, and 1.2x zoom, which is why it shows up in compact golf simulator bays as well as conference rooms.

Who it's for

The MW632ST is a fit if you need a bright short-throw picture in a garage, basement, or office and do not require native 1080p. Native resolution is WXGA (1280 x 800) at 16:10, which can work well on some simulator screens but is not Full HD. If you want sharper course textures or a 16:9 1080p image, a 1080p model is the clearer choice.

It also suits setups that need flexible placement. The zoom, short throw, auto vertical keystone, and dual HDMI ports make it easier to sit the projector close to the hitting area, or to switch between a computer and a second device, without a lot of extra adapters.

Key features

Details below come from BenQ’s official U.S. MW632ST specifications:

  • DLP projector with 3,200 ANSI lumens and native WXGA resolution (1280 x 800), 16:10 native aspect ratio, and a listed contrast ratio of 13,000:1.
  • Short-throw lens with a 0.72–0.87 throw ratio and 1.2x zoom. Lens specs are listed as F/2.56–2.68, f=22.04–24.14 mm, with a 105% projection offset.
  • Auto vertical keystone correction of +/- 30 degrees.
  • Two HDMI inputs, plus VGA in and out, composite and S-video, USB Type-A, USB mini-B, 3.5 mm audio in and out, and a 10-watt speaker.
  • Lamp light source with rated life of 4,000 hours in Normal, 6,000 in ECO, 6,500 in SmartEco, and 10,000 in LampSave.
  • Resolution support from VGA up to WUXGA (1920 x 1200, reduced blanking). Horizontal frequency 15–102 kHz; vertical scan rate 23–120 Hz.
  • Dimensions listed at 287.3 x 114.4 x 232.6 mm and a net weight of 2.6 kg. A remote, power cord, and VGA cable are included accessories on BenQ’s spec sheet.

How to use it

Set the projector in front of the screen and use the throw ratio and 1.2x zoom to fill the image area without sitting so far back that you throw a shadow. In a golf bay, mount or place it so a swing and follow-through stay out of the lens path. Connect your simulator PC or other source to one of the HDMI ports, then use auto vertical keystone only as needed; a square, level mount will look cleaner than a heavy keystone adjustment.

Choose a picture mode that keeps the screen bright enough in your room. Garage bays often need more brightness than a dark theater, which is where the 3,200-lumen rating helps, though ambient light and screen material still matter. Use LampSave or ECO modes if you want longer lamp life once you have enough brightness. The built-in speaker is a backup, not a substitute for room audio.

Because the native image is 16:10 WXGA, you may see letterboxing or scaling on a 16:9 or 4:3 impact screen. Test the layout before you permanently mount the projector.

Quick FAQ

What resolution does it use?

Native resolution is WXGA, 1280 x 800, with a 16:10 aspect ratio. It can accept other resolutions, including up to WUXGA input, but it is not a native 1080p projector.

How short is the throw?

The throw ratio is 0.72 to 0.87, with 1.2x zoom, so it can throw a large image from a relatively short distance compared with a long-throw classroom projector.

How many HDMI ports are there?

Two HDMI inputs, plus VGA and several legacy video and audio connections.

How long does the lamp last?

BenQ rates lamp life from 4,000 hours in Normal mode up to 10,000 hours in LampSave mode.
