A yoke changes how a flight sim feels: you hold the aircraft the way a pilot does, pull back to rotate, and make small corrections on final instead of nudging a stick. The Logitech G PRO Flight Yoke System pairs a yoke built on a stainless steel shaft with a separate three-lever throttle quadrant, so you get pitch, roll, and power controls in one box for Windows PC flight simulation.

Who it's for

Logitech describes this system as precise and realistic for both beginner and advanced simulation. It suits PC simmers flying Microsoft Flight Simulator, X-Plane, or Prepar3D who want a yoke and throttle together rather than buying them separately, and who fly aircraft where a yoke makes sense, from light singles to airliners.

It also fits people who plan to grow their setup. The yoke has a built-in USB hub for additional Logitech G Flight hardware, and additional throttle quadrants are sold separately for multi-engine aircraft. Note that it is a PC product; check the Amazon listing for current operating system support.

Key features

Details below come from Logitech G's product page and support specifications for the Flight Yoke System:

  • Yoke with a durable stainless steel elevator and aileron control shaft and precision bearings, for smooth, predictable control.
  • Five total axes across the yoke and the included throttle quadrant.
  • Included Flight Throttle Quadrant with three levers. All levers are programmable, and the lever knobs are detachable, so you can configure any combination of throttle, flaps, mixture, or propeller pitch.
  • Lever detents that help with idle and reverse thrust control.
  • Three two-way toggle switches on the throttle quadrant base.
  • 25 programmable buttons and switch positions in total. A three-position mode switch on the yoke cycles through three profiles, for up to 75 user-definable controls with Logitech's software.
  • Built-in stopwatch for timing approaches, and an integrated USB hub.
  • The throttle quadrant connects to the yoke, and the yoke connects to the PC over USB.
  • Desk clamps included, so the yoke and quadrant can sit on top of a desk or clamp to the front edge.

How to use it

Clamp the yoke to the front edge of your desk, centered on your main screen, and make sure it has space for the full in-and-out travel of the shaft. Place the throttle quadrant to the right for a single-pilot feel, or wherever your hand naturally falls; Logitech's clamps let you mount it on top of the desk or in front of it. Plug the quadrant into the yoke, then connect the yoke to a USB port on your PC.

Open your sim's controls menu and check that pitch, roll, and the three levers are assigned correctly. For a single-engine piston aircraft, a common layout is throttle, propeller, and mixture; for a jet, you might use the levers for throttles and flaps or spoilers. Because the knobs are detachable, swap them to match the aircraft you fly most.

Use the toggle switches for things like landing gear or lights, and the hat switch for views. If you want more controls than the buttons allow, install Logitech's software and set up profiles on the mode switch. Pair the system with rudder pedals for proper coordinated flight and crosswind landings.
