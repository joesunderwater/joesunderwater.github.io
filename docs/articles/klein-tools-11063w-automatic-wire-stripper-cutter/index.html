Stripping wire with a basic stripper means finding the right notch, squeezing, twisting, and pulling, over and over. An automatic stripper turns all of that into a single squeeze. The Klein Tools 11063W grips the wire and pulls the insulation off in one motion, and it has a cutter built in so you don't have to swap tools. It's on the Power Tools and DIY board as a time-saver for anyone who does more than the occasional bit of wiring.

Who it's for

Klein describes this as a tool for electrical work, and it suits:

  • Electricians and trades pros who strip a lot of wire in a day and want one-handed operation.
  • DIYers and homeowners working on outlets, switches, light fixtures, and similar projects (with the power off).
  • Hobbyists wiring projects in a garage or workshop who want clean strips without nicking the conductor.

One important note from Klein: this tool is not insulated and should never be used on or near live circuits.

Key features

These details come from the Amazon listing and Klein Tools' own product page for model 11063W:

  • Automatic, one-squeeze stripping. One squeeze of the handles grips the wire and strips the insulation, so you can work one-handed.
  • Wire range. Strips 8–20 AWG solid wire and 10–22 AWG stranded wire.
  • Integrated cutter. The built-in cutter handles up to 8 AWG solid wire.
  • Precision-machined stripping holes. Klein says they remove up to 1 inch of insulation in a single step without damaging the wire.
  • Self-opening spring. An internal spring opens the handles for repetitive stripping.
  • Cast alloy chassis. Klein lists a cast alloy body with a heavy-duty e-coat finish.
  • Dual-material grips. Ergonomic grips for comfort and control during longer jobs.
  • Size. Klein lists an overall length of about 6.6 inches and a weight of about 12 ounces.

How to use it

Klein's instructions describe a simple process:

1. Make sure the circuit is off. The tool is not insulated. 2. Place the wire across the jaws at the knife opening that matches its gauge. 3. Let the end of the wire stick out past the cutting knives as far as you want to strip, up to 1 inch. 4. Squeeze the handles quickly. The knives engage and pull the insulation off. 5. Release the handles. The jaws open and let go of the stripped wire.

Klein notes that clear nylon-jacketed conductors need two squeezes. To cut, use the cutter section of the jaws.

For care, clean insulation scraps out of the tool after use, check that the cutting knives stay tight, and oil the pivot points and moving parts now and then. Klein says lack of lubrication can shorten the tool's life.

What wire sizes does it strip?

Klein lists 8–20 AWG solid and 10–22 AWG stranded wire. (The pin text mentions 10–18 AWG; the manufacturer's stated range is wider.)

Can it cut wire too?

Yes. The integrated cutter handles up to 8 AWG solid wire.

Is it safe to use on live wires?

No. Klein says the tool is not insulated and should never be used on or near live electrical circuits. Wear eye protection too.
