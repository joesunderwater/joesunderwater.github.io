How to use it

Start by finding a steel surface. A quick test with any fridge magnet tells you whether a spot will work; aluminum, stainless steel that isn't magnetic, wood, and drywall won't hold these hooks.

A few ideas that match the brand's own suggestions:

1. Kitchen: put two or three on the side of the fridge for aprons, oven mitts, dish towels, or a utensil or two. 2. Entryway: stick one on a steel front door or a metal shelf for keys, a dog leash, or a hat. 3. Garage and workshop: hang light tools, extension cords, or work gloves on a metal rack, tool chest, or steel beam. 4. Office: add a hook to a filing cabinet for a bag, headphones, or a lanyard. 5. Travel: pack a few for a cruise cabin to keep towels and swimsuits off the floor.

Keep loads realistic. Because pull strength drops on painted, thin, or textured metal and when a hook takes a sideways load, it's smart to start light and add weight gradually instead of hanging something heavy right away.
