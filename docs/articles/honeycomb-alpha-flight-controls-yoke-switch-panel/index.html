Flying a light aircraft with a joystick or keyboard works, but it never quite feels like sitting in the left seat. The Honeycomb Alpha Flight Controls Yoke & Switch Panel is built to close that gap: a full-size yoke with 180 degrees of rotation on a solid steel shaft, plus a switch panel in the base for the master, alternator, avionics, lights, and ignition. It is designed for home flight simulation on PC and Mac in Microsoft Flight Simulator, X-Plane, and Prepar3D.

Who it's for

Honeycomb aims the Alpha at flight simmers, flight students, and pilots who want a realistic yoke for hand-flying at home. It is a natural pick if you mostly fly general aviation aircraft, practice pattern work and approaches, or want to run through startup and shutdown flows with physical switches instead of clicking around a virtual cockpit.

It is also a fit for people building a desk setup in stages. The Alpha covers pitch and roll plus a core set of panel switches, and you can add rudder pedals or a throttle quadrant later, since flight sims let you assign separate USB controllers.

Key features

Details below come from Honeycomb's product page and the Amazon listing for this yoke:

  • Realistic 180-degree yoke rotation.
  • Solid steel shaft running on dual linear ball bearings, for smooth, precise pitch and roll input.
  • Damped, self-centering mechanism with no center detent and minimal dead zone.
  • Integrated switch panel in the base with master, alternator, avionics, and light switches.
  • A 5-position ignition switch for realistic engine start and magneto checks.
  • Red LED backlighting on the panel for visibility during night flights.
  • Fully equipped handles: the left handle has an 8-way POV hat switch, two vertical 2-way rocker switches, buttons, and a push-to-talk button; the right handle has two horizontal 2-way rocker switches and a small and a large programmable button.
  • Dual Mounting Solution, so the yoke can be secured to a desk or table.
  • USB-C connection, with a USB-C to USB-A cable included.
  • Compatible with PC and Mac, and with X-Plane, Prepar3D, and Microsoft Flight Simulator.

How to use it

Mount the yoke at the front edge of your desk using the included mounting solution, centered on your monitor so the yoke sits where a real one would be in front of you. Leave enough room behind the base for the full pull-back travel of the shaft. Connect it to your computer with the included USB-C cable.

In your sim's controls menu, confirm that pitch and roll are assigned to the yoke, then map the switch panel to the matching aircraft functions: battery master, alternator, avionics, and lights. The ignition switch is worth setting up early, since turning it through the magneto positions and holding it at start makes engine runups feel much closer to the real thing.

The rocker switches on the handles are a natural home for elevator and aileron trim, and the hat switch works well for views. Assign the push-to-talk button if you fly with ATC or online networks. Because every button and switch is programmable, you can change the layout per aircraft. Add rudder pedals when you are ready for coordinated turns and crosswind landings.

Quick FAQ

Does it work with Microsoft Flight Simulator?

Yes. Honeycomb lists compatibility with Microsoft Flight Simulator, X-Plane, and Prepar3D on PC and Mac. Check the Amazon listing for the latest compatibility notes.

How far does the yoke turn?

The yoke has a realistic 180 degrees of rotation, with a damped, self-centering mechanism and no center detent.

What switches are on the panel?

The base includes master, alternator, avionics, and light switches, plus a 5-position ignition switch, all with red LED backlighting.

Does it include a throttle?

No. The Alpha is a yoke and switch panel. Throttle, mixture, and other levers come from a separate throttle quadrant or other controller.
