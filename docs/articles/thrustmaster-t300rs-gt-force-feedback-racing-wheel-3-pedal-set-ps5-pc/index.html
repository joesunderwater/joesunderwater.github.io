Once a starter wheel starts to feel vague, the usual next step is a smoother, more detailed force-feedback base and a proper three-pedal set. The Thrustmaster T300RS GT is built for that step: a belt-driven wheel with a brushless motor, official PlayStation and Gran Turismo licensing, and the T3PA-GT three-pedal set with metal pedal heads.

Who it's for

The T300RS GT is for sim racers who want a quieter, more refined mid-range wheel than an entry-level gear-driven set, and who race on PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, or PC. Thrustmaster also lists PlayStation 3 compatibility. It is officially licensed for PlayStation and Gran Turismo, which makes it a common choice for Gran Turismo 7 as well as PC titles such as Assetto Corsa.

It is also a fit if you want a wheel you can grow. The T300RS series uses a swappable rim system, so you can change to other compatible Thrustmaster wheels later, and the ecosystem includes optional pedals, shifters, and a handbrake. A solid desk clamp or cockpit is still important; belt-driven feedback is smoother than many entry wheels, but the base still needs a stable mount.

Key features

Details below come from Thrustmaster’s official T300RS GT Edition product materials:

  • Force-feedback racing wheel for PC, PS3, PS4, and PS5, officially licensed by PlayStation and Gran Turismo.
  • Fast brushless servomotor, described as a 25W motor that stays quiet and holds consistent force-feedback intensity during long sessions, unlike a basic brushed motor.
  • Dual-belt drive system, designed to smooth force-feedback effects, reduce dead zones, and avoid the harsh jolts of some gear-driven wheels while still transmitting power to the rim.
  • Contactless H.E.A.R.T Hall-effect magnetic technology on the wheel, specified at up to 16-bit resolution (65,536 values), with no potentiometer in that sensor path.
  • Swappable wheel system shared with other T300RS-series models, so you can fit compatible Thrustmaster rims as your racing style changes.
  • T3PA-GT three-pedal set with metal heads for the accelerator, brake, and clutch. The accelerator and clutch offer adjustable height and spacing, and the brake uses progressive resistance with a conical rubber brake mod.
  • A wide official game-compatibility list on PlayStation and PC. Thrustmaster publishes a current mapping download for Gran Turismo 7, which is worth installing rather than assuming an out-of-box setup is final.

How to use it

Mount the base securely, attach the GT rim, and place the T3PA-GT pedals so the brake can take firm pressure without sliding. On PlayStation, set the USB switch to the correct console position and press the wheel’s PS button so the system recognizes it. On PC, install Thrustmaster’s current drivers and firmware before you spend time on in-game calibration.

Start with a mid-level force-feedback strength and 900 degrees of rotation in games that support it, then tune the brake mod and pedal spacing until the pedal set feels consistent. The progressive brake is there so you can modulate pressure rather than treat the pedal like a switch. If you race several disciplines, the swappable rim is the upgrade path: a GT-style wheel for road cars, and a different official add-on later for rally or open-wheel cars.

Keep an eye on official game lists and GT7 mapping updates, especially after a game patch. If you later add a Thrustmaster shifter or load-cell pedals, confirm they are listed as compatible with the T300RS base.
