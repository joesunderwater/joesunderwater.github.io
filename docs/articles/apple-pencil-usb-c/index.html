If you already carry an iPad, adding a stylus is the simplest way to turn it into a notebook, a sketchbook, and a planner at the same time. Apple Pencil (USB-C) is Apple's entry point into the Pencil lineup. Apple describes it as offering pixel-perfect precision, tilt sensitivity, and low latency for note taking, signing documents, and drawing, and it pairs and charges over USB-C instead of a separate adapter.

Who it's for

This Pencil suits students who want handwritten notes on their iPad, people who run their week in a digital planner, and anyone who signs or marks up PDFs often. It also works for casual sketchers and hobby artists who want to draw, color, and doodle without buying a dedicated drawing tablet.

The most important thing to check is compatibility. Amazon titles the listing "Device Compatibility Check Required," and Apple lists specific models that work with it, including:

  • iPad Pro 13-inch (M4 and M5) and iPad Pro 11-inch (M4 and M5)
  • iPad Pro 12.9-inch (3rd through 6th generation) and iPad Pro 11-inch (1st through 4th generation)
  • iPad Air 13-inch (M2, M3, M4), iPad Air 11-inch (M2 and M3), and iPad Air (4th and 5th generation)
  • iPad (A16) and iPad (10th generation)
  • iPad mini (A20 Pro and A17 Pro) and iPad mini (6th generation)

If your iPad is not on that list, look at the compatibility notes on the Amazon listing before you buy.

Key features

  • Pixel-perfect precision. Apple says you can write, sketch, and draw with pixel-perfect precision.
  • Tilt sensitivity. Angle the Pencil to change your stroke, which is handy for shading in drawing apps.
  • Low latency. Apple highlights low latency so lines appear quickly as you write.
  • USB-C pairing and charging. The Pencil pairs and charges through USB-C.
  • Magnetic flat edge. It has a flat edge that attaches magnetically to your iPad, so it stays with the tablet.
  • Hover on supported iPads. On iPad Pro and iPad Air, Apple Pencil hover lets you preview your mark before you make it.
  • Works across apps. Use built-in apps like Notes and Freeform, or the many drawing, note-taking, and planning apps on the App Store.
  • In the box: Apple Pencil (USB-C).

How to use it

Pair the Pencil over USB-C, then snap it to the side of your iPad so it is ready whenever you open the case. For school or meetings, open Notes and write directly on the page, then mark up slides or PDFs as you go. For planning, load a digital planner template into your favorite note app and use the Pencil to check off tasks and block out your week.

If you like to draw, Freeform is a free place to start sketching ideas. Tilt the Pencil for broader shading strokes, and if you have a supported iPad Pro or iPad Air, use hover to see where your mark will land before touching the screen. Signing documents is quick too: open the PDF, sign with the Pencil, and send it back without printing.
