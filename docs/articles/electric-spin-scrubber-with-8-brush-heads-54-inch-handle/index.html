Scrubbing a tub or shower floor by hand usually means kneeling on hard tile and working your arms until they ache. An electric spin scrubber puts a motor behind the brush so the spinning does most of the work, and a long handle keeps you standing. Who it's for

This scrubber is aimed at people who dread scrubbing tubs, shower walls, tile, and grout, and at anyone who would rather not bend or kneel while cleaning. The brand pitches the long handle as a way to save your back and knees.

Because it comes with several head types, it also suits people who want one tool for more than the bathroom. The listing names floors, walls, pools, windows, wood floors, furniture, and even car polishing.

Key features

These details come from the Amazon listing for this ASIN:

  • 8 replaceable brush heads. The set includes a flat brush for tile floors, walls, and pools, a corner brush for grout, faucets, and edges, a cloth head for wood floors and furniture, and a sponge for windows.
  • Adjustable handle from 12 to 54 inches. Use it short as a handheld scrubber, or add the telescopic extension rod to reach high walls and low floors without kneeling.
  • Two speeds. The listing gives 300 RPM on low and 400 RPM on high, switched with the on/off button.
  • USB-C charging. Leebein says about 3 hours of charging gives up to 90 minutes of use, and there is a battery level display.
  • IPX7 waterproof rating. The brush is sealed so you can rinse it under the faucet. The listing notes that it should not be used submerged under water.
  • Support. The listing mentions a 90-day refund window and a 2-year extended protection plan from the brand.

How to use it

Charge the scrubber fully before the first use. Choose the head for the job: the flat brush for big areas like a tub floor or tile wall, the corner brush for grout lines and around faucets, and the sponge for glass.

Spray your usual bathroom cleaner on the surface and let it sit for the time on its label. Then turn on the scrubber, start on the low speed, and move slowly so the spinning brush can work. Switch to high for stubborn buildup. Attach the extension rod when you need to reach the top of the shower walls or the floor without crouching.

When you finish, rinse the head and the brush under the tap, let them dry, and recharge. Keep in mind it is waterproof for rinsing, not for use underwater, so don't dunk it in a full tub.
