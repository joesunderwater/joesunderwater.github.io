Who it's for

The G923 is a fit if you want a first serious sim racing wheel that works on a PlayStation and a PC, without jumping straight into a direct-drive base. Logitech positions it as a next-generation belt-driven-style racing system with TRUEFORCE feedback that reads in-game physics in real time. It is a natural match for Gran Turismo 7, F1 titles, and PC sims, provided the game supports wheels.

It is also aimed at people who want a three-pedal set from the start, including a clutch, rather than a two-pedal beginner wheel. The set is designed to clamp to a desk or mount to a cockpit; a sturdy table or a racing stand will make the force feedback more usable than a light folding desk.

Key features

Details below come from Logitech’s official G923 product and support materials for the PlayStation and PC version:

  • Racing wheel and three-pedal set for PS5, PS4, and Windows PC, connected over USB.
  • TRUEFORCE high-definition force feedback, which Logitech describes as connecting directly to in-game physics so you can feel shifts, slides, and surface changes. Official materials also list 2.3 Nm of torque and closed-loop motor control that adjusts voltage to match the game’s output.
  • Up to 900 degrees of wheel rotation.
  • Hand-stitched leather-wrapped wheel with a center marker, a rotary dial, RPM/shift-indicator LEDs, and steel or aluminum paddle shifters.
  • Three pedals: throttle, brake, and clutch. The brake is a pressure-sensitive progressive design, intended to make trail braking feel more natural than a simple on-off pedal.
  • Programmable dual-clutch launch assist in supported games, for cleaner standing starts.
  • Customization and settings through Logitech G HUB on PC. Check the current software notes for console configuration options.

How to use it

Clamp the wheel base firmly to a desk or bolt it to a cockpit, then place the pedal set so you can use full brake pressure without sliding the unit. Connect it over USB, set the console or PC to recognize a racing wheel, and calibrate steering and pedals in the game’s control menu. On PlayStation, follow Logitech’s prompt to use the PS button on the wheel so the system sees it correctly.

Start with a modest force-feedback strength and the default rotation, then raise the feedback as you get used to TRUEFORCE effects. The progressive brake is the pedal to practice first: apply pressure gradually rather than stomping, especially on trail braking into a corner. If a game supports the dual-clutch launch assist, set that up for standing starts rather than guessing at throttle-and-clutch timing.

G HUB on PC is the place to update firmware and adjust wheel settings. Not every title uses every effect the same way, so use the in-game wheel menu as well. If you later add a shifter or move to a dedicated stand, confirm compatibility with this PlayStation/PC version of the G923, which is different from the Xbox model.

What is TRUEFORCE?

It is Logitech’s high-definition force-feedback system, designed to take data from a game’s physics and translate it into detailed wheel effects such as curbs, slips, and engine texture.

How many pedals are included?

Three: throttle, brake, and clutch. The brake uses a progressive, pressure-sensitive design.

The wheel is built for PS5, PS4, and PC racing titles, including games such as Gran Turismo 7 and F1, when those titles support racing wheels. Check each game’s current wheel support if you have a specific title in mind.
