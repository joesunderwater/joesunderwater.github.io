Hanging a TV, a heavy mirror, or a row of shelves always starts the same way: finding the studs. Old-school stud finders make you swipe back and forth, recalibrate, and guess where the edge really is. The Franklin Sensors ProSensor M210 takes a different approach, with a row of sensors that shows the whole stud at once. It is on the Power Tools and DIY board because it takes a lot of guesswork out of one of the most common jobs around the house.

Who it's for

The M210 is aimed at anyone who drills into drywall: homeowners hanging shelves and art, renters mounting a TV (where allowed), and DIYers framing out a project. Franklin positions it against conventional electronic stud finders with one or two sensors and against magnetic finders that only locate screws. If you have ever drilled three holes to find one stud, this is the kind of tool that can save some patching.

Key features

These details come from the Amazon listing for this ASIN:

  • 13 sensors. Franklin says conventional electronic stud finders have one or two sensors, while the M210 has 13 for more accuracy.
  • Shows the full width of studs. The LED display lights up across the whole stud, including double studs, and the middle LED turns green to mark the stud's center.
  • No calibration. Press and hold the button to start scanning. Franklin says you can start anywhere, even right over a stud.
  • Deep scan always on. The listing says detection reaches up to 1.7 inches and finds both wood and metal studs.
  • Live wire detection. The M210 includes live AC wire detection.
  • Built-in bubble level and pencil caddy. Handy for marking a level line once you have found your studs.
  • Power. It takes two AA batteries, which are not included. The listing says it is compatible with rechargeable batteries.

How to use it

1. Add batteries. Pop in two AA batteries (bring your own, since they are not in the box). 2. Press and hold. Place the M210 flat on the wall, hold the button, and slide it slowly along the wall. No calibration step is needed. 3. Read the lights. The LEDs light up over the full width of the stud. When the middle LED turns green, you are over the center. 4. Mark it. Use the pencil caddy to mark the stud edges or center, then use the built-in bubble level to carry a level line across to the next stud. 5. Watch for wires. Pay attention to the live wire detection before you drill, and take extra care around outlets and switches.

For shelves and TV mounts, find and mark every stud across the span first, then plan your bracket holes around those marks. It is faster than finding one stud, drilling, and hoping the next one is 16 inches over.
