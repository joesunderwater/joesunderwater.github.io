Who it's for

The Quest 3S fits sim racers and virtual pilots who want to try VR without a headset that only works tethered to a PC. You can play standalone Quest games anywhere, then connect to your PC when it is time for a racing or flight session. Meta lists PC support through the Meta Quest Link cable and Air Link (sold separately).

For sims, the pin points to iRacing, Assetto Corsa, and Microsoft Flight Simulator on PC. VR in those games runs on your computer, not the headset, so check each title's VR support and your PC's specs before you buy. It also suits glasses wearers: Meta includes a glasses spacer in the box.

Key features

Details below come from Meta's official Quest 3S specifications and the Amazon listing for this ASIN:

  • 128GB of storage on this listing, with 8GB of RAM. Meta says that is 33% more memory than Quest 2.
  • Snapdragon XR2 Gen 2 processor. Meta says it delivers twice the GPU processing power of Quest 2.
  • Display: 1832 × 1920 pixels per eye, with 72Hz, 90Hz, and 120Hz refresh rates, Fresnel lenses, and three-position lens spacing (IAD) adjustment.
  • Full-color passthrough from 2 RGB cameras, so you can see your room and mix virtual objects into it.
  • Hand tracking for controller-free navigation, plus Meta Quest Touch Plus controllers with TruTouch haptics.
  • PC VR support with the Meta Quest Link cable or Air Link (sold separately).
  • Audio: built-in spatial audio, plus support for USB-C headphones.
  • Comfort: a soft adjustable strap and an included glasses spacer. Meta lists the headset at 514 grams.
  • Battery: Meta says up to 2.5 hours of average use, about 1.9 hours to charge with the included 18W adapter, and Wi-Fi 6E where available.
  • In the box (per the Amazon listing): headset with facial interface, glasses spacer, two Touch Plus controllers with AA batteries, two wrist straps, power adapter, and charging cable.

Bundle extras on this listing, such as included games or trial memberships, can change. Check the Amazon page for what currently comes with it.

How to use it

Charge the headset, pair it with the Meta Horizon app on your phone, and run through setup, including your play area. Get used to standalone VR for a few sessions before jumping into a sim. If you wear glasses, fit the spacer, and use the lens-spacing settings to get a clear image.

For PC sims, install the Meta Quest Link software on your computer, then connect with a Link cable for a wired setup or use Air Link over a strong Wi-Fi connection. Launch your racing or flight sim in VR mode and recenter your view once you are seated in the cockpit. Because your wheel, stick, or yoke is hidden while the headset is on, place your controls where your hands find them easily. For long sim sessions on battery, keep the 2.5-hour average in mind.
