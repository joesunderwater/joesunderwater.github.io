Who it's for

This feeder is for cat and dog households that want scheduled dry-food meals without a smartphone app. It suits people who work irregular hours, take short trips, or simply want breakfast, lunch, dinner, and a snack to run on a clock. It also fits multi-pet homes that share one bowl, with the usual caveat that a single-bowl feeder cannot tell pets apart.

It is designed for dry kibble only. The listing says not to use wet, semi-moist, sticky, or oily food, which can clog the dispenser. Kibble that flows smoothly, in about the 2–10 mm range, is what it is built for.

Key features

These details come from the product listing and brand information for this 4L VOLUAS model:

  • 4-liter hopper. The tank holds about 16.9 cups of dry food, enough to cover several days for many cats or small dogs.
  • 1 to 4 scheduled meals a day. You set meal times on the built-in clock and LCD screen.
  • Portion control from 0 to 40. Each portion is described as about 6–7 grams, or roughly a tablespoon. A manual feed button can also drop a small extra serving.
  • Dual power. It runs from the included 5V USB adapter and cable, with a compartment for three D-cell batteries (not included) as backup during an outage.
  • 10-second voice recording. You can record a short mealtime call so your pet hears your voice when food drops.
  • Removable stainless-steel bowl and bowl holder, plus a desiccant bag for the hopper.
  • Size. About 7.4 x 7.4 x 11.8 inches.
  • What's in the box. The feeder, USB cable, power adapter, instruction manual, desiccant bag, stainless-steel bowl, and bowl holder.

How to use it

Set the feeder on a level floor, fit the bowl and holder, and fill the hopper with dry kibble that fits the size range in the manual. Program the clock, then choose how many meals you want and how many portions each meal should drop. It is worth measuring a test portion in a cup the first day so you know what those 0–40 settings actually look like with your brand of food.

Plug it into the USB adapter for everyday use, and add the three D-cell batteries if you want backup during a power cut. Record a short voice call if you like, then lock the controls so the schedule cannot be changed by a curious paw. Wash the hopper, tray, and lid by hand as the manual directs, and keep the desiccant bag out of the water. The bowl is the part meant to come out for regular cleaning.
