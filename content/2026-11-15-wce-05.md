## Who it's for

The Kindle Scribe fits people who think better with a pen: managers who take notes in every meeting, students, writers, and anyone who keeps a journal. It also suits people who review a lot of documents and would rather mark them up by hand than print them out.

Readers get a lot here too. The large 11-inch display gives you more room on the page, and you can write notes directly in books from the Kindle Store. Because it is an e-reader and notebook rather than a full tablet, Amazon pitches it as a device with no distractions.

## How to use it

Before a meeting, import the agenda or a draft from Google Drive or OneDrive. Mark it up with the pen during the discussion, then export it as a PDF to share. For general notes, use the built-in notebook and let the AI tools summarize what you wrote or convert your handwriting to text afterward. If your team uses OneNote, you can send notebooks there to edit anywhere.

For journaling or planning, the paper-like surface and pen with a built-in eraser make it feel close to a paper notebook, without the stack of filled pages.

For reading, the warm-light setting is useful at night, and you can jot thoughts right on the page of a Kindle book. On a plane or in a hotel, it can stand in for a notebook, a folder of documents, and a few books.

## Quick FAQ

### Does the pen need charging?

No. Amazon says the included pen never needs charging. It has a built-in eraser and a shortcut button.

### Can I write on PDFs and work documents?

Yes. You can import files from Google Drive or Microsoft OneDrive, add handwritten notes and markups, and export them as PDFs.

### Can it convert my handwriting to text?

Yes. The built-in notebook includes tools to clean up handwriting or convert it to text, as well as AI features to search and summarize your notes.

### Is it good for reading at night?

The display has a front light that adjusts to your surroundings, and you can make the light warmer for nighttime reading.
