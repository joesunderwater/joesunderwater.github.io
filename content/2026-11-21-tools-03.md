## How to use it

An impact driver feels different from a drill. When the fastener meets resistance, the tool starts hammering in short rotational bursts, which is what helps it drive long screws without twisting your wrist. A few tips:

- **Use impact-rated bits.** Standard bits can wear quickly under impact. Pick 1/4 inch hex bits rated for impact use.
- **Change bits with one hand.** Pull the quick-release collar, drop in a bit, and let go.
- **Ease into the trigger.** Start slowly to seat the screw, then squeeze harder once it's biting. This helps avoid overdriving into softer wood.
- **Let the LED help.** It's handy when you're working under a deck, inside a cabinet, or in a dim garage corner.
- **Pre-drill when needed.** For hardwoods or near the edge of a board, a pilot hole from your drill can help prevent splitting.

Charge the battery fully before your first job, and store the driver, battery, and charger together so they're easy to find.
