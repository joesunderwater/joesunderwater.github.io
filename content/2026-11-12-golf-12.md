## Who it's for

This mat is for golfers who mostly hit off the turf — irons, wedges, fairway woods, and chips — and want a 4 x 5 ft stance-and-hitting surface that is lighter and more rollable than a thick commercial mat. All Turf Mats makes the turf in Dalton, Georgia, and specifies nylon because it resists the heat of club strikes better than many budget polyethylene mats and is not supposed to streak the club with green residue.

It is not the right choice if you want to push a real wooden tee into the turf. All Turf Mats is clear that Fairway mats have a tight 3/8-inch pile and work with rubber tees only. This 4x5 5 mm foam version includes pre-punched rubber tee holes and a rubber tee for driver and fairway-wood practice. If you prefer wooden tees, the company’s Super Tee line is the alternative.

## How to use it

Unroll the mat on a flat floor and give it time to relax, or set it in a warm spot to help it flatten. Stand and hit from the turf for irons, wedges, and chips. For driver or fairway woods, use the included rubber tee in one of the pre-punched holes rather than a wooden tee. Place a net or screen in front, and keep the mat from sliding if your floor is especially slick.

Because the lie is tight, the mat rewards clean contact. That makes it useful for iron practice, but it will feel different from a longer tee-box turf. Rotate hitting spots to spread wear, and clean the nylon with a hose or pressure washer as the company recommends. When you need the room, roll it up; the 5 mm foam is the backing All Turf Mats suggests for that kind of storage.
