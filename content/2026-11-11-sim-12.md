If you race from a couch or a desk chair, the weak point is usually the mount, not the wheel: a clamp that twists, a pedal tray that slides, or a chair that rolls away under braking. The GT Omega Apex is a foldable steel wheel stand meant to fix that, with a Y-frame, adjustable wheel and pedal plates, and a Chair Link cradle included so a wheeled office or gaming chair can stay put.

## Who it's for

The Apex is for people who want a dedicated wheel-and-pedal mount without giving up a room to a full cockpit. GT Omega presents it as a compact, foldable stand you can use with a sofa, an office chair, or a gaming chair, on PlayStation, Xbox, or PC. It is a fit if you need to store the rig between sessions, because the stand is designed to fold with the wheel and pedals still attached.

It is also a practical match if you want a shifter mount and a chair-stop included rather than sold separately. ## How to use it

Assemble the frame, then set the wheel-deck height and angle so the wheel sits in a natural reach from your chair. Adjust the pedal-plate angle next: a steeper plate often works better with a higher seat, while a flatter angle can suit a lower couch or bucket-style sit. Hard-mount the wheel and pedals to the pre-drilled plates when your hardware allows it, and use the included mounting hardware that matches your pattern.

If your chair has casters, put those wheels into the Chair Link before you start a session so a hard brake input does not roll you away from the pedals. A chair without wheels may not need the cradle, but it still needs a stable position against the stand. Mount a shifter on the left or right if you use one, then tighten every adjustment before you raise force feedback or brake pressure.

When you are finished, fold the stand as the official instructions show, leaving the wheel and pedals attached if you want a faster setup next time. If you later want a dedicated seat, GT Omega sells separate seat-frame add-ons; those are not part of this listing. Check the current mounting-hole diagram for your exact wheel base rather than assuming every direct-drive unit uses the same pattern.
