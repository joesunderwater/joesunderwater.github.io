## How to use it

Start low and work up. With the dial, you can begin at a gentle temperature for delicate materials and raise it only as needed. That matters most for plastics, vinyl-like materials, and 3D prints, which can warp or scorch fast.

Pick the nozzle for the job:

- **Reflector nozzle** for heat-shrink tubing, so the heat wraps around the tube evenly.
- **Concentrator nozzles** when you want heat on one small spot, like a single solder joint area or a stubborn bolt.
- **Deflector nozzle** for a wide, flat pattern when softening paint on a board or trim.

Keep the gun moving rather than holding it in one spot, and keep the nozzle a little distance from the surface. When you're done, let the gun cool on a stable, heat-safe surface before storing it, because the nozzle stays hot for a while. Always follow the safety directions in the included manual, especially around flammable materials and older painted surfaces.
